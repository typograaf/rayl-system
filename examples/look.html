The Rayl look

A treatment, not a component

One elliptical radial gradient under a named effect style, applied as a modifier so that it can go on a card, a panel or a row without any of them changing. The intent, from the frame itself: cards that organise information, scrollable and interactive, reinforced by a skeuomorphic design.

Measured on 966:2041 and verified on 966:1980 and 966:2014. Every colour on this page is named. Nothing in the treatment is a hex.

Two things in RAYL-WHY's reading of the effect style are wrong and both were caught by rendering this beside the frame rather than beside the numbers. Figma paints its effect list later-over-earlier and CSS paints the first shadow on top, so the list has to be reversed — copied straight down, the grey lands over the white and the bottom edge comes out dark instead of lifted. And the offsets are negative, so the grey presses in from the bottom-right, not the top-left.

The colour: three complete re-strikes

Not one of the five values on the frame sits on the palette's arc. Every one of them is under-chromatic for its lightness — the treatment was built in grey and the re-strike is putting the hue back. The centre is not really a choice: RAYL-WHY already names #CECEC5 as the value rayl-screen drifted into and Porcelain as the correction. What is a choice is the rim, and the inset ground follows it.

I would take A

Depth alone does not decide it: against the drawn card on White, A overshoots by 2.0 L* and B undershoots by 1.9. Three things decide it.

The comparison that matters is like for like. The drawn card sits on the board's own sheet and its bowl is 5.7 L* there. A card on a Rayl page sits on surface/ground, and option A's bowl there is 5.5. B gives 1.6 and C gives 4.5. A reproduces what the card actually looks like where it actually lives, to within 0.2 L*.

B loses the gradient. 1.6 L* across a 330px card is not a bowl, and a treatment whose first sentence is "one elliptical radial gradient" should not be one you have to be told about.

The rim and the inset are one step. The drawn rim and the drawn inset ground are 1.1 L* apart — they were never two colours, they are one, separated by the shadow rather than by the fill. Bone is 0.8 L* off both. Dark Off-White is 2.0 and 3.1 off. A keeps the 108 inset rows on the step they are drawn on.

The one thing A costs: Bone is the colour selection is painted in — surface/active and surface/idle-pressed both resolve to it. A card permanently the colour of a selected control is a real objection. It is weakened by the card being a gradient rather than a flat Bone, and by the treatment sitting on surfaces rather than controls, but it is the reason to choose B if you choose B.

Chroma is OKLCh C x 1000. "The arc wants" is the palette's own chroma at that lightness. Every drawn value is under it, which is one finding rather than six: the treatment was mixed in neutral grey. #808061 is the exception in the other direction — chroma 44.9 against the palette's maximum of 20.1, and hue 107.70 where every palette colour sits between 106.5 and 106.9. It is the only value on the frame that is off the hue as well as off the arc.

The two greys land on the same step because they are 1.0 L* apart. They were never two colours. White stays White: it is on the palette, and it is the documented achromatic exception rather than a member of the arc, which is exactly what a specular lift wants to be.

Dark — a proposal, with no reference behind it

Nothing was drawn for this. What follows was chosen by matching each effect's L* delta rather than by flipping a token, and two of the three deltas cannot be matched, because the palette's dark end is compressed: below surface/ground there are two steps, where above it in light there are six.

The rim holds. In light the card sits 6.6 L* below the page; Black under Soft Black is 4.9 below. Close enough to be the same relationship.

The bowl does not. 2.5 L* against light's 5.5, and there is no pair of steps that does better without the rim meeting the ground. The dark card is flatter than the light one and no choice of palette colour fixes that.

The lift survives at its measured alpha. In light, White at 85% takes the bottom edge up about 5.6 L*. Soft Black at 85% on a Black rim takes it up about 4.9. Same gesture, same number, different colour — which is the one place the inverse works.

The press does not, and this is the invented part. In light the top-left corner darkens by roughly 12 L*. In dark there are 5.2 L* of headroom between the rim and Deep Black in total, so the corner cannot darken by 12 at any alpha. The proposal raises the press from its measured 30% to 70% and still only gets about 3.3. That is one measured number changed, and the alternative is to drop the press in dark and let the bottom lift carry the whole reading on its own. I have not chosen between them.

The class names

classwhat
is-lookthe treatment: the gradient, three inner shadows, rounding 24. Goes on anything.
is-look is-insetthe same shadow on a solid ground, rounding 8 — the small row inside a card
is-look is-deepthe fourth inner shadow switched on. Off everywhere in the file; a decision, not a fix.

A modifier rather than a component, and named the way every other modifier in the system is named — is-strong, is-joined, is-tight. It is what lets rayl-card, rayl-panel, rayl-frame and a bare div all take it without any of them being restyled, and it is what lets the opt-in and the blanket answer both be expressible.
