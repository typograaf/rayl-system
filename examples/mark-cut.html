The mark in motion

The same turn, cut instead of squashed.

The same puzzle as the other one, and the same turn: a layer still sweeps round its axis, the near pieces swinging wide and the far ones coming into view behind them. What is different is what gets drawn while it does. There it is a face squashed by perspective; here the turn only says how wide a face has become, and the shape is cut again at that width with its corners still round — so a circle narrows into a capsule and then into a line, and never into an ellipse. Nothing is scaled and nothing is 3D. A face on its way to edge-on still passes through being a hairline; ten answers to that are laid out side by side, along with a switch for holding the whole turn inside the square.

What you are watching

Every piece still carries its own shape on every one of its faces, so a bar can arrive standing up and a bar seen end-on is a circle. Mid-turn a piece shows two of them at once, the one turning away and the one coming round, each cut to its own width. Everything is ink at full strength, and the back of the cube stays hidden behind the ground each piece carries with it.

Easing
Anything CSS takes: cubic-bezier, linear, ease-in-out, steps

Paste an easing and it takes effect on the next turn. Everything is in milliseconds, and the four fields are the whole rhythm: how long a turn takes, the beat between two of them, the quicker turn it scrambles at, and how long it sits on the solved mark before scrambling again.

The default is the system's own — cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1) over 300ms, the same curve and duration every Rayl control moves on.

One puzzle drives all four. This version holds together smaller than the other does — a shape cut narrow keeps its corners at any size, where a squashed one loses them.

The rules it plays by
The pieces

Six: two bars two cells wide, and four corners. They fill eight cells at the icon's own pitch, so the gaps between them are the gaps in the mark.

The turns

Any layer you can see, a quarter or a half at a time, as long as it does not run through a bar. A turn about the upright cuts the shapes narrower as they swing; one about the level cuts them shorter; the front layer turns on the spot and is cut not at all.

The solve

Two thousand and sixteen positions exist and none is more than ten turns from home, so it knows the shortest way back from all of them. Sometimes it takes a turn that is not on it, thinks, and finds the way again.
