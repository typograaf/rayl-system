The mark in motion

Smaller the further out it has gone.

Of the first ten, Floor and Pebble were the two worth keeping. What both were missing is that the pieces on their way out held the same size as each other the whole way. So the size now follows the position rather than the clock: a piece sitting over its own cell is full size, and the further a turn has pushed it past the edge of the square, the smaller it is. The piece nearest the edge is always the smallest one, the piece behind it is next, and the ones coming back in grow as they arrive — nothing is the same size as anything else until everything is home. Below, Floor and Pebble as they were, then each of them with the falloff at two strengths and on a curve.

The square
Holding it in

Free lets a turn swing wide of the mark, which is what a cube does. Held in scales the whole mark down for as long as the turn needs the room and lets it back up again — nothing is lost, but the mark breathes. Cropped keeps it at size and cuts off whatever leaves the square, which holds the size still and loses the corners of the swing.

Easing
Anything CSS takes: cubic-bezier, linear, ease-in-out, steps

Opens on cubic-bezier(0.85, 0, 0.15, 1) with everything at 1500, which is where you left it. That curve holds still at both ends and crosses the middle quickly, which is exactly the part of a turn these ten differ in — worth putting back to 300 once you have picked one, to check it still reads at speed.
