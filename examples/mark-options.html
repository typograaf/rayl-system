The mark in motion

Ten ways to keep a turn from going thin.

The trouble with the cut version is the end of a turn: a face on its way to edge-on passes through being a hairline, and a hairline looks like it might snap. Here is the same puzzle driving ten answers to that at once, so they can be compared turn for turn rather than described. The switch below also holds every one of them inside the square, which none of them do on their own — a turn swings a piece half a cell wider than the mark it belongs to.

Motion
The square
Holding it in

Free lets a turn swing wide of the mark, which is what a cube does. Held in scales the whole mark down for as long as the turn needs the room and lets it back up again — nothing is lost, but the mark breathes. Cropped keeps it at size and cuts off whatever leaves the square, which holds the size still and loses the corners of the swing.
