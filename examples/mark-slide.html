The mark in motion

Sliding, a cell apart, smaller toward the edge.

The same puzzle as the turning one and the same shapes as the cut one, with three rules and nothing else in it. Where: a lane's four pieces slide along one line, always a cell apart, one cell per quarter turn, the far one coming back round to the near side — not where a turn would project them, because a turn puts its pieces on a circle and shows you that circle from the side, so their spacing closes up and opens out as it goes round. What shape: the piece still turns on its own centre and the face toward you is cut again at the width that leaves it, corners staying round. How big: the further out it stands the smaller, and gone by the edge — read off its position and nothing else, so a piece moving out shrinks as it goes and the piece behind it shrinks when it reaches the same place.

What you are watching

One shape per piece, only ever the face most nearly square to you. Two faces at once is what needed a ground tile behind each piece to hide what was behind it, and those tiles are what were cutting into things. Nothing overlaps here, so nothing needs hiding: a cell of pitch is wider than anything that can stand in it, and no piece is ever drawn bigger than the room it has left, which is also why nothing is ever clipped.

Easing
Anything CSS takes: cubic-bezier, linear, ease-in-out, steps

Paste an easing and it takes effect on the next turn. Everything is in milliseconds, and the four fields are the whole rhythm: how long a turn takes, the beat between two of them, the quicker turn it scrambles at, and how long it sits on the solved mark before scrambling again.

It opens on cubic-bezier(0.85, 0, 0.15, 1), which holds still at both ends and crosses the middle quickly — the system's own curve, cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1), is the gentler version of the same idea and is what every Rayl control moves on.

One puzzle drives all four. This version holds together smaller than the other does — a shape cut narrow keeps its corners at any size, where a squashed one loses them.

The rules it plays by
The pieces

Six: two bars two cells wide, and four corners, over eight places — the four you can see and four off the sides. The pitch is the icon's own, so the gaps between them are the gaps in the mark, at every moment.

The turns

Four lanes slide — the two rows and the two columns — a cell at a time, as long as the lane does not run through a bar. The face itself turns on the spot, which is the one move that reorients a bar rather than moving it along.

The solve

Two thousand and sixteen positions exist and none is more than ten turns from home, so it knows the shortest way back from all of them. Sometimes it takes a turn that is not on it, thinks, and finds the way again.
