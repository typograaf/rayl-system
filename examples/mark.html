The mark in motion

The icon is the solved state.

Three shapes on a two-by-two grid, so the mark is the front of a two-by-two cube. The bar is one piece two cells wide and is never cut, which means a layer can only turn when it does not run through it — the turns on offer change as the cube moves. It scrambles itself, works out the shortest way back, takes the occasional wrong turn, and lands on the mark exactly. The cut version makes the same turns without a third dimension.

What you are watching

Every piece carries its own shape on every one of its faces — a bar is a bar on all four of its long faces and a circle on the two ends, the way a capsule sections — so nothing is ever cut and no face is ever blank. Everything is ink at full strength — no second tone, nothing at part opacity — and there is no background: each shape sits on a tile of the ground a whole cell wide, so the back of the cube is hidden the way a solid hides what is behind it.

Easing
Anything CSS takes: cubic-bezier, linear, ease-in-out, steps

Paste an easing and it takes effect on the next turn. Everything is in milliseconds, and the four fields are the whole rhythm: how long a turn takes, the beat between two of them, the quicker turn it scrambles at, and how long it sits on the solved mark before scrambling again.

It opens on cubic-bezier(0.85, 0, 0.15, 1), which holds still at both ends and crosses the middle quickly — the system's own curve, cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1), is the gentler version of the same idea and is what every Rayl control moves on.

The rules it plays by
The pieces

Six: two bars two cells wide, and four corners. They fill eight cells at the icon's own pitch, so the gaps between them are the gaps in the mark.

The turns

Any layer you can see, a quarter or a half at a time, as long as it does not run through a bar. The back layer is left alone — nothing in it is drawn, so turning it would be a turn nobody could watch.

The solve

Two thousand and sixteen positions exist and none is more than ten turns from home, so it knows the shortest way back from all of them. Sometimes it takes a turn that is not on it, thinks, and finds the way again.
