The mark in motion

The icon is the solved state.

Three shapes on a two-by-two grid, so the mark is the front of a two-by-two cube. The bar is one piece two cells wide and is never cut, which means a layer can only turn when it does not run through it — the turns on offer change as the cube moves. It scrambles itself, works out the shortest way back, takes the occasional wrong turn, and lands on the mark exactly.

What you are watching

Every piece carries its own shape on every one of its faces — a bar is a bar on all four of its long faces and a circle on the two ends, the way a capsule sections — so nothing is ever cut and no face is ever blank. What changes as a piece turns is the tone: square to you it is ink, every other way it is a rung of the greyscale, and the further back it sits the further that rung is mixed into the ground.

Easing
Anything CSS takes: cubic-bezier, linear, ease-in-out, steps

Paste an easing and it takes effect on the next turn. Everything is in milliseconds, and the four fields are the whole rhythm: how long a turn takes, the beat between two of them, the quicker turn it scrambles at, and how long it sits on the solved mark before scrambling again.

The default is the system's own — cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1) over 300ms, the same curve and duration every Rayl control moves on.

The rules it plays by
The pieces

Six: two bars two cells wide, and four corners. They fill eight cells at the icon's own pitch, so the gaps between them are the gaps in the mark.

The turns

Any of the six layers, a quarter or a half at a time, as long as the layer does not run through a bar. Twelve turns are on offer while the two bars lie the same way, six once they do not.

The solve

Two thousand and sixteen positions exist and none is more than nine turns from home, so it knows the shortest way back from all of them. Sometimes it takes a turn that is not on it, thinks, and finds the way again.
