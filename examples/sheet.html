The sheet

Every sheet on this page is in a box. The controls below drive them. Move the pointer over one and the light leans after it.

The reference's own shader, read off its running page rather than described: a ramp of three colours across the frame, a curtain whose height is simplex noise drifting in two directions, and a wide soft disc of a fourth colour under the cursor. Their numbers, measured on the page: time at one a second, amplitude 1, blend 0.5, the disc reaching 0.6 of the frame and taking the colour 30 per cent and the alpha 50. The pointer is not smoothed, because theirs is not.

One thing is not theirs, and it is the whole of what makes this Rayl. Their ramp, their curtain and their cursor disc all carry colours, and they composite over a ground through an alpha; here every one of them carries a level between nought and one instead, and the colour is named once at the end. Nought is the gradient's dark stop and one is its light stop, and the number cannot leave that range — so the brightest pixel on a sheet is the brightest colour the gradient has, the darkest is its darkest, and there is nothing outside it to blow out. The canvas is opaque: the ground is inside the shader as a number rather than under a translucent layer, so nothing here is drawn at part strength either.

Section 7 says gradients are flat art and stay flat. This drifts on its own and answers the pointer. The canvas carries alpha and composites over the ground, the way the reference does, which is the second thing in the system drawn at part strength. Still is the value that obeys the rules, and it is what a reader who has asked for reduced motion gets: no canvas, and the flat approved gradient underneath.

The same thing in a box, and in a small one

Everything in the shader is in fractions of the box it is in, so a 96 high strip reads the way a full screen does. All three follow the controls. The gradient button drives the big one only — the small pair keeps one each, so the two are still side by side to compare.
